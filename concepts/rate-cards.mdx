A rate card is the pricing layer between your app features and Jaza credits. It is a simple map of feature keys to positive integer credit costs. When your backend calls `jaza.consume()`, Jaza looks up the feature in your app's rate card, verifies the wallet has enough credits, and deducts the exact amount.

## What a rate card is

A rate card is an app-level dictionary where each key is a feature string and each value is a credit cost. Feature keys are strings you define, so you can name them whatever matches your product domain. Costs must be positive integers because Jaza does not support fractional credits.

## Feature key naming

Use descriptive, uppercase strings so they read clearly in dashboards and transaction history. Examples include `SEND_SMS`, `AI_PROMPT`, `PRINT_RECEIPT`, and `GENERATE_REPORT`. These names appear in the Jaza dashboard and in ledger entries, so clarity benefits both you and your customers.

## How rate cards work

- **Per-app scope**: A rate card belongs to your Jaza app, not to individual users. All customers in the same app are charged the same credit price for a given feature.
- **Instant updates**: When you change a rate card, the new cost takes effect immediately on the next `consume()` call. There is no propagation delay.
- **Enforced at consume time**: Jaza rejects a `consume()` call if the feature does not exist in the rate card or if the wallet lacks sufficient credits.

## Example rate card

```json
{
  "SEND_SMS": 2,
  "AI_PROMPT": 10,
  "PRINT_RECEIPT": 3,
  "GENERATE_REPORT": 5
}
```

With this rate card, calling `jaza.consume({ customerId: 'cus_123', feature: 'AI_PROMPT' })` costs 10 credits. Calling the same method with `feature: 'SEND_SMS'` costs 2 credits.

<Tip>
  Use descriptive feature names. They appear in the Jaza dashboard, transaction history, and ledger entries, so `AI_PROMPT` is clearer than `F1`.
</Tip>
