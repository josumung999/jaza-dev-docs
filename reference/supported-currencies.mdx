Jaza is built for African payment rails, so you work in local currencies with no floating-point rounding surprises. Every amount is an integer in the local currency unit (for example, `100` for 100 KES, not `1.00`). This keeps wallet balances and rate cards consistent across you, your customers, and the ledger.

## Supported currencies and payment methods

| Currency | Code | Country | Payment Methods |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kenyan Shilling | KES | Kenya | M-Pesa, Card |
| Congolese Franc | CDF | DR Congo | Airtel Money |
| Ghanaian Cedi | GHS | Ghana | MTN MoMo, Card |
| Nigerian Naira | NGN | Nigeria | Airtel Money, Card |
| Ugandan Shilling | UGX | Uganda | MTN MoMo, Airtel Money |
| Rwandan Franc | RWF | Rwanda | MTN MoMo |
| Tanzanian Shilling | TZS | Tanzania | Airtel Money |

## Currency availability by gateway

<Tip>
  Not every gateway supports every market in every country. Currency availability ultimately depends on your PawaPay or payment gateway coverage in the region. If you need a corridor that is not listed here, contact Jaza support.
</Tip>

## Pass amounts as integers

<Note>
  Always pass top-up and consume amounts as integers in the local currency. For example, to top up 500 KES, send `500` in the `amount` field, not `500.00` or `5.00`.
</Note>

Using integer amounts avoids floating-point rounding issues entirely. Wallet balances, rate cards, and the immutable ledger all store exact integer values.

## USD pricing

USD is not a primary currency in Jaza. Pricing is anchored in local currencies so your customers see familiar, stable amounts tied to their local economy. If you need cross-currency reporting, reach out to Jaza support for options.
